Cut Your Summer Water Bill in Holladay: Smart Controllers + Drip MOCK

22s ago · irrigation
Summer is when Utah water bills spike — and a surprising amount of that water never does your yard any good. It evaporates off a midday lawn, runs onto the sidewalk, or soaks the fence instead of the flower bed. Two upgrades fix most of that: a smart controller and drip irrigation where it makes sense. A wifi smart controller waters in the early morning, automatically dials back when the weather is cool or wet, and lets you tweak everything from your phone. Drip irrigation delivers water slowly and directly to the roots of beds, shrubs, and gardens — almost none is lost to evaporation or overspray. Together they keep things green while cutting the water you're paying for. For a Holladay homeowner this week, it was a one-morning upgrade with savings they'll see all summer.

Frequently asked

What's the best time to water in a Utah summer?
Early morning — generally before about 9 a.m. It minimizes evaporation and wind loss and lets the lawn dry during the day, which is healthier than evening watering.
What is a smart controller?
A wifi-connected sprinkler timer that adjusts watering based on local weather and season, and that you can control from an app. Many Utah water districts even offer rebates for installing one.
How much water can drip irrigation save?
Drip is dramatically more efficient than spray for beds and gardens because it targets the roots. For the right zones it's one of the biggest water-savers we can install.

Spring Sprinkler Start-Up in Sandy: What to Check Before the First Hot Week MOCK

22s ago · irrigation
If you live in Sandy or anywhere along the Salt Lake valley, the few weeks before summer are the best time to get your sprinkler system checked. Pipes that sat through a Utah winter can crack, valves can stick, and heads get knocked out of alignment over the off-season — and you usually don't notice until you've got a brown patch or a water bill that doubled overnight. A proper spring start-up is more than just turning the water on. We slowly pressurize the system to avoid water hammer, walk every zone to check spray patterns and coverage, replace cracked or tilted heads, check valves and the backflow assembly, and set the controller for the season so you're not watering at noon in July. Twenty minutes of attention in spring prevents most of the emergency calls we get in the heat of summer.

Frequently asked

When should I turn my sprinklers on in Utah?
Usually mid-to-late spring, once the overnight lows are reliably above freezing. In the Salt Lake valley that's typically April into May. Starting up too early risks a freeze cracking a line.
What's included in a spring start-up?
We pressurize the system safely, check every zone for coverage, replace broken or misaligned heads, inspect valves and backflow, and program the controller for efficient, water-wise watering.
Do you repair systems you didn't install?
Absolutely — most of the systems we service we didn't install. We work on every common brand of head, valve, and controller.
