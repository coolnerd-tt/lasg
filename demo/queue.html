Spring Sprinkler Start-Up in Sandy: What to Check Before the First Hot Week
If you live in Sandy or anywhere along the Salt Lake valley, the few weeks before summer are the best time to get your sprinkler system checked. Pipes that sat through a Utah winter can crack, valves can stick, and heads get knocked out of alignment over the off-season — and you usually don't notice until you've got a brown patch or a water bill that doubled overnight. A proper spring start-up is more than just turning the water on. We slowly pressurize the system to avoid water hammer, walk every zone to check spray patterns and coverage, replace cracked or tilted heads, check valves and the backflow assembly, and set the controller for the season so you're not watering at noon in July. Twenty minutes of attention in spring prevents most of the emergency calls we get in the heat of summer.
